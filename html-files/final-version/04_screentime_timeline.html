Stress increased

▼

We can conclude that there is clearly a very strong correlation between increased screen time and negative impacts on mental health. Whether it is sleep issues , restlessness or emotional distress, the data consistently shows that users who report spending more than 5 hours daily on social media experience significantly higher levels of mental health challenges.
This includes increased attention problems, heightened emotional distress and stronger behavioral dependence habits.

Anxious?

▼

We can conclude that long screen time can be contributed to decreased emotional wellbeing. Users who spend more time on social media report higher levels of anxiety and worry. However, there's a slight decrease between users who spend 3-5 hours (3.90) and users who spend 4-5 hours (3.69).

Seeking validation?

▼

Interestingly, the social validation group suggests increase with longer screentime, but this difference is much less pronounced compared to the other metrics. For comparison the attention metric experiences a jump from 2.01 to 3.82, while social validation only increases from 2.27 to 2.85. This could suggest that while social media may contribute to feelings of comparison and validation seeking, these effects might not escalate as dramatically with increased usage as issues regarding shortened attention span.

Dependent?

▼

After Attention & Distraction and Behavioral Dependence experience the largest increase with longer screentime. This suggests that users who spend more time on social media are not only more likely to experience attention problems but also develop stronger behavioral dependence on these platforms. Key factors of this metric is respondents reporting restlessness and sleep issues.
